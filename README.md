
#  The Economic Ripple Effect of Food Waste
### UKM Data Challenge 5.0 | Team Kaw Kaw Penguins

##  Project Overview

**The Economic Ripple Effect of Food Waste** is a data analytics project developed for **UKM Data Challenge 5.0**, exploring the relationship between food waste, household expenditure, inflation, and economic sustainability in Malaysia.

The project aims to uncover the hidden financial costs of food waste and demonstrate how reducing household food waste can contribute to financial savings and sustainable consumption.

Using statistical analysis, time series forecasting, and an interactive Power BI dashboard, we explored historical trends, regional differences, and future projections of food waste in Malaysia.

---

##  Project Objectives

- Quantify the amount and financial impact of food waste across Malaysian states.
- Analyse the relationship between household income, food expenditure, inflation, and food waste.
- Examine whether rising food prices influence food waste patterns.
- Forecast future trends in food waste, food expenditure, and the Consumer Price Index (CPI).
- Promote awareness of potential household savings through food waste reduction.

---

##  Key Insights & Findings

### 🗑️ 1. Malaysia's Growing Food Waste Problem

- Approximately **43.9 billion kg of food waste** was estimated across the 2014–2024 analysis period.
- Food waste accounted for approximately **44.4% of household waste composition**.
- Food waste generation showed an increasing trend over the years, highlighting the need for more sustainable consumption practices.

###  2. States Contributing the Most Food Waste

- **Selangor, Johor, and Sabah** were identified as the highest food-waste-generating states.
- **Perlis, Putrajaya, and Labuan** recorded the lowest estimated food waste.
- Differences in population size and household consumption patterns may contribute to variations across states.

###  3. The Inflation–Food Waste Paradox

- Rising food prices and inflation did not correspond with a reduction in estimated food waste.
- Food waste continued increasing despite growing financial pressure on households.
- The findings suggest that consumer behaviour, food storage, and purchasing habits may play important roles in food waste generation.

###  4. Understanding Food Waste Behaviour

The analysis highlighted several common reasons for household food waste:

- Food exceeding its expiration date.
- Food spoilage and improper storage.
- Food no longer being fresh.
- Over-purchasing and poor meal planning.

These findings highlight the importance of behavioural changes alongside economic interventions.

###  5. Future Forecast: Looking Towards 2034

Based on the project's time series projections:

- **5.1 billion kg** of food waste could be generated annually by 2034.
- Monthly food expenditure could reach approximately **RM948**.
- The Consumer Price Index (CPI) is projected to increase by approximately **37.67 index points** over 10 years.

These projections illustrate the potential economic consequences if existing consumption and waste patterns continue.

##  Interactive Food Waste Savings Calculator

One of the key features of our Power BI dashboard is an interactive **Food Waste Cost & Savings Calculator**.

Users can input:

1. Monthly household food expenditure.
2. Estimated monthly food waste (kg).
3. Their desired food waste reduction target (%).

The calculator estimates:

- Monthly and annual food waste costs.
- Potential monthly savings.
- Potential annual savings.

This feature encourages users to view food waste not only as an environmental issue but also as a form of financial loss.

---

##  Power BI Dashboard Features

Our interactive dashboard consists of six main pages:

1. **National Food Waste** – Malaysia's waste generation trends and household waste composition.
2. **State Food Waste** – Geographical comparisons of food waste across Malaysian states.
3. **CPI & Inflation** – Relationships between food prices, inflation, and food waste.
4. **Household Expenditure** – Household income and food spending comparisons.
5. **Food Waste Calculator** – Interactive estimation of food waste costs and potential savings.
6. **Forecasting** – Future projections of food waste, expenditure, and CPI.

##  Conclusion

Our analysis highlights that **food waste is not just an environmental concern but also an economic issue**.

Despite rising food prices, household food waste remains significant, suggesting that better purchasing decisions, food storage practices, and public awareness could help reduce unnecessary financial losses.

By combining data analytics, forecasting, and interactive visualisation, this project demonstrates how data-driven insights can support more sustainable and financially responsible consumption habits.
